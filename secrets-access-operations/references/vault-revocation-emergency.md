# Vault revocation, sessions and emergency access

Read `vault-boundary.md` first. Preserve a separately controlled recovery administrator path and record the affected identity, issuer/auth method, policies, token tree, dynamic leases, cached static values and downstream sessions. Immediate containment still needs explicit authority for its blast radius.

## Revoke effective authority

1. Identify service tokens through protected accessor references. Use `POST /auth/token/revoke-accessor` for the exact token; normal revocation also removes descendants and associated leases. Inventory independently issued/orphan tokens separately. `revoke-orphan` preserves children and is not complete containment. Batch tokens cannot be revoked individually: select their parent/issuer/policy containment with the owner or track their fixed expiry.
2. For a dynamic credential, target its exact lease through `POST /sys/leases/revoke`. A queued acceptance is not completed revocation: use `sync=true` where supported and separately prove backend denial/session closeout. Prefix revocation can remove unrelated consumers; require separately bounded prefix scope. A failed backend revocation is residual access, not success. `revoke-force` discards Vault lease tracking even when backend cleanup failed; it is not proof of revoked credentials and is not a repair shortcut.
3. Close reissuance paths separately: disable/restrict the exact IdP principal, auth-role binding, AppRole SecretID or workload identity as appropriate through its owner. Deleting a login credential/role does not prove existing tokens revoked, and revoking a token does not prevent a valid identity logging in again. Never disable an entire auth mount to resolve one token without separate blast-radius approval.
4. Coordinate target-native session termination and cache/pool invalidation with the database/cloud/service owner. Vault can revoke its token/lease while a previously authenticated backend session persists. Rotate copied KV/static credentials at their real provider. Prove old Vault access and renewal are denied, fresh issuance is denied where containment requires it, affected backend credentials fail and pre-existing sessions are closed. Verify unaffected consumer health and recovery access too.

Record completed, failed and unknown steps separately; retain an exact residual-access list and deadline. An unreachable provider, missing audit records or an HTTP error without an authorization result cannot prove revocation. See [tokens](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/docs/concepts/tokens), [token API](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/api-docs/auth/token), [leases](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/docs/concepts/lease), and [lease administration](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/api-docs/system/leases).

## Emergency grant and closeout

1. Bind the incident, accountable owner, independently authenticated approvals, named executor, exact emergency actions and expiry to the operation. Check monitoring, audit sink, recovery channel and an independent revocation/watchdog owner before issuance. An outage is not authority to fabricate an approver or use a shared standing root token.
2. Prefer a pre-established restricted emergency auth role and a finite, nonperiodic service token with an explicit maximum lifetime. Deliver only to the approved attributable session. Verify emergency actions and denied unrelated actions; monitor the bounded session and do not broaden policies when one step fails.
3. If root is truly required and ordinary auth is unavailable, hand the exceptional `sys/generate-root` ceremony to designated quorum custodians. Verify cluster/attempt nonce, threshold, custodian identities and protected OTP or PGP handling. No shares, OTP, encoded output or root token may pass through the agent. Recovery shares are for the supported recovery/generation workflow, not a replacement for unseal keys. Cancel an abandoned attempt through its owner.
4. A generated root token is not automatically a short-lived grant. Require explicit immediate revocation after the narrowly approved recovery, an independently monitored deadline/kill path and proof that root and any derived grants are unusable. Before issuance establish enforced expiry or a tested independent revocation/watchdog path; if neither is available, stop and hand off. An incident deadline alone does not enforce expiry.
5. Close the session, revoke emergency/derived tokens and leases, restore temporary auth/policy changes, rotate any exposed/used bootstrap references according to their real owner, and test old denial plus normal access. Record remaining backend sessions/unknowns and obtain independent post-use review. Quorum/recovery/root handling is not exercised by the dev-server CI fixture.

See [generate-root API](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/api-docs/system/generate-root) and [root generation](https://developer.hashicorp.com/vault/docs/commands/operator/generate-root). Root recovery never bypasses the platform's target, authority, recovery or evidence requirements.
